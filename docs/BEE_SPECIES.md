# Bee & Pollinator Species — Buzzing Meadows

This document catalogs every pollinator species planned for discovery in-game.
All biological facts are accurate to real-world science.

---

## How to Read This Document

- **Rarity Tiers:** Common / Uncommon / Rare / Seasonal
- **Attraction:** What the player must do to encounter this species
- **Educational Fact:** 1–2 sentences of accurate biology

---

## Species Catalog

---

### 1. Western Honeybee
**Scientific Name:** *Apis mellifera*  
**Family:** Apidae  
**Rarity:** Common  

**Habitat & Range:** Worldwide (originally from Africa/Europe); now found on every
continent except Antarctica. Highly adaptable to temperate, subtropical, and tropical
climates when managed by beekeepers.

**In-Game Attraction:**
- Present from the start (managed hive)
- Season: Spring, Summer, Fall
- Climate: All zones
- Hive type: Langstroth, Top Bar, Warré, Flow Hive

**Educational Fact:** The Western Honeybee is the only bee species that produces
honey in quantities large enough for commercial harvest. A single healthy colony can
contain 50,000–80,000 bees at peak summer population and may collect up to 100 kg
of nectar in a single season.

### 2. Common Eastern Bumblebee
**Scientific Name:** *Bombus impatiens*  
**Family:** Apidae  
**Rarity:** Common  

**Habitat & Range:** Eastern North America, from Canada south through the eastern
United States and into parts of Mexico. One of the most widespread bumblebee species
in North America.

**In-Game Attraction:**
- Plant clover, lavender, or bee balm in the garden
- Season: Spring, Summer, Fall
- Climate: Temperate Midwest, Southeastern US, Pacific Northwest

**Educational Fact:** Unlike honeybees, bumblebee colonies are annual — a new queen
overwinters alone and founds a new colony each spring. *Bombus impatiens* is also
the primary commercial pollinator for greenhouse tomatoes, using buzz pollination
(sonication) to vibrate pollen loose from tomato anthers.

### 3. Blue Orchard Mason Bee
**Scientific Name:** *Osmia lignaria*  
**Family:** Megachilidae  
**Rarity:** Uncommon  

**Habitat & Range:** Western and eastern North America; prefers open woodlands and
orchards. Active very early in spring when most other bees are not yet flying.

**In-Game Attraction:**
- Install a mason bee house (wooden block with 5/16" holes) in the garden
- Season: Early Spring only (Seasonal)
- Climate: Temperate Midwest, Pacific Northwest

**Educational Fact:** Mason bees are solitary; each female constructs her own nest,
partitioning tubes with mud "walls" — hence the name "mason." A single mason bee
is estimated to be 100× more efficient as a pollinator than a honeybee for orchard
crops like apples and cherries because she carries dry pollen on her abdomen and
loses far more per flower visited.

### 4. Alfalfa Leafcutter Bee
**Scientific Name:** *Megachile rotundata*  
**Family:** Megachilidae  
**Rarity:** Uncommon  

**Habitat & Range:** Native to Eurasia; introduced to North America as a managed
pollinator for alfalfa seed production. Now naturalized across much of the continent.

**In-Game Attraction:**
- Plant alfalfa or other legumes
- Season: Summer
- Climate: Temperate Midwest, Desert Southwest

**Educational Fact:** Leafcutter bees cut precise semicircular pieces from leaves
(especially roses and beans) to line their brood cells — the plant is not harmed,
only slightly cosmetically damaged. The female uses these leaf pieces to form a
cigar-shaped roll around each egg and its pollen provision.

### 5. Ligated Sweat Bee
**Scientific Name:** *Halictus rubicundus*  
**Family:** Halictidae  
**Rarity:** Common  

**Habitat & Range:** Holarctic distribution — widespread across North America, Europe,
and Asia. Highly adaptable; found in gardens, meadows, and disturbed habitats.

**In-Game Attraction:**
- Present in any garden area with exposed bare soil (nesting sites)
- Season: Spring, Summer, Fall
- Climate: All zones

**Educational Fact:** Sweat bees are named for their attraction to human perspiration,
which provides salts and moisture. They are primitively eusocial — some populations
are solitary while others form small colonies with a queen and workers, depending on
local climate and season length.

### 6. Squash Bee
**Scientific Name:** *Peponapis pruinosa*  
**Family:** Apidae  
**Rarity:** Uncommon  

**Habitat & Range:** North America, closely tracking the range of cultivated
Cucurbita squash. Distribution has expanded with the spread of agriculture.

**In-Game Attraction:**
- Plant squash, pumpkin, or zucchini in the garden
- Season: Summer (early morning only — Seasonal behavior)
- Climate: Temperate Midwest, Desert Southwest, Southeastern US

**Educational Fact:** Squash bees are highly specialized (oligolectic) — they collect
pollen exclusively from squash flowers (Cucurbita). Males often sleep inside closed
squash blossoms overnight. Studies show squash bees may be responsible for more
squash pollination than honeybees in many regions.

### 7. Eastern Carpenter Bee
**Scientific Name:** *Xylocopa virginica*  
**Family:** Apidae  
**Rarity:** Common  

**Habitat & Range:** Eastern North America from Canada to Florida and west to the
Rocky Mountains. Prefers wood structures and old timber for nesting.

**In-Game Attraction:**
- Leave unpainted wooden fences or structures in the farm area
- Season: Spring, Summer
- Climate: Temperate Midwest, Southeastern US

**Educational Fact:** Carpenter bees excavate perfectly round (1/2") nest tunnels
in soft wood, turning 90° to tunnel along the wood grain. Despite their large size
and buzzy flight, only females can sting and rarely do. Males (identifiable by a
yellow patch on their face) are completely harmless but territorial, often hovering
near humans.

### 8. Monarch Butterfly
**Scientific Name:** *Danaus plexippus*  
**Family:** Nymphalidae  
**Rarity:** Seasonal (Rare in late summer/fall)  

**Habitat & Range:** North America; famous for multi-generational migration between
Mexican overwintering grounds and summer breeding range across the United States
and Canada.

**In-Game Attraction:**
- Plant milkweed (*Asclepias* spp.) — the only host plant for Monarch caterpillars
- Season: Late Summer, Early Fall (migration timing)
- Climate: Temperate Midwest, Desert Southwest (migration corridor)

**Educational Fact:** Monarch butterflies are the only known butterfly to make a
true two-way migration like birds. The eastern population travels up to 4,500 km
to overwinter in oyamel fir forests in Michoacán, Mexico. Milkweed is essential —
the caterpillars feed exclusively on it, and the plants are being lost to herbicide
use and habitat conversion across the Midwest.

### 9. Painted Lady Butterfly
**Scientific Name:** *Vanessa cardui*  
**Family:** Nymphalidae  
**Rarity:** Common (highly variable year to year — Seasonal)  

**Habitat & Range:** The most widespread butterfly in the world; found on every
continent except Antarctica and South America. In North America, populations build
up in the Mojave Desert and migrate north each spring.

**In-Game Attraction:**
- Plant thistles, asters, or coneflowers
- Season: Spring, Summer
- Climate: All zones

**Educational Fact:** Unlike Monarchs, Painted Ladies do not return south — each
generation lives and dies in their summer range, and new generations migrate north
from Mexico and the Southwest the following year. Population explosions occur every
few years when winter rains in the Mojave produce spectacular wildflower blooms,
resulting in billions of butterflies moving through California and the Midwest.

### 10. Hoverfly (Flower Fly)
**Scientific Name:** *Syrphidae* family (multiple genera)  
**Family:** Syrphidae (flies, not bees)  
**Rarity:** Common  

**Habitat & Range:** Worldwide. Nearly 6,000 species globally; common in gardens,
meadows, and woodland edges wherever flowers are present.

**In-Game Attraction:**
- Any flowering plant will attract hoverflies
- Season: Spring, Summer, Fall
- Climate: All zones

**Educational Fact:** Hoverflies are flies (Diptera), not bees — but many species
mimic bee and wasp coloration for predator protection (Batesian mimicry). Despite
this deception, hoverflies are important secondary pollinators, ranking second only
to bees in overall pollination contribution in some studies. They cannot sting.
